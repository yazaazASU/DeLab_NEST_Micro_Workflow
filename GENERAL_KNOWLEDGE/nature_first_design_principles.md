## 1. Our Vision: Hidden Potential in Egypt

### Hidden
A place is *hidden* not necessarily because nobody knows about it, but because a valuable aspect of its natural, cultural, social, or economic system has not been fully understood, connected, taken into consideration, or responsibly utilized—even though data exists for it or can be predicted from the nearest available value (within a maximum **2.5 km** radius). It is a natural place with an unusual phenomenon, ecological relationship, geological feature, or environmental process whose value is not widely recognized.

> **NEST Directive:** Remain open to any natural phenomenon, including those not yet recognized as architecturally or economically valuable.

### Potential
The capacity of an unusual natural characteristic, biological process, ecological system, or landscape condition to inspire and support an innovative architectural intervention that creates scientific, cultural, social, environmental, or economic value.

> **NEST Directive:** The discovery process must be driven by evidence, observation, scientific investigation, and unconventional thinking—not limited to a fixed list of natural features.

### Economic Base
The capacity of a site's unique and underrecognized natural characteristics to generate high, sustainable financial returns through innovative architectural interventions, diversified revenue streams, and strategic investment models.

> **NEST Directive:** Evaluate this potential using economic forecasting, mathematical models, market analysis, and funding strategies to identify the most profitable and financially resilient architectural opportunities over time. Calculate the required investment, expected revenue, profitability, payback period, long-term financial performance, and potential funding partners. Investigate unconventional sources of value even when no existing market or architectural precedent is immediately apparent. NEST's role is to discover, test, and develop profitable opportunities—not simply select from familiar building types.

### Balance with Nature
The strategic integration of architectural development with existing natural systems, where preserving biodiversity, ecological processes, and distinctive environmental features directly supports the project's economic value and long-term profitability. Architecture should generate financial benefits through the continued health and uniqueness of nature, without degrading the ecological assets on which its success depends. **Nature is not just something we avoid damaging—it is the project's primary economic asset.**

> **NEST Directive:** Seek opportunities where ecological preservation strengthens financial value, and where a portion of the economic benefits supports the continued health of the natural system. Whenever a financially feasible option compromises ecological integrity, flag it explicitly as **`Harmful to the Environment`** and propose a nature-safe alternative if one exists.

### Overarching Architectural Theme: Integrating Nature, Not Just Inspired By Nature *(Mandatory Concept Standard)*
Every building concept produced by NEST must move beyond superficial biomimicry, nature-themed styling, or cosmetic "inspired by" forms. **Architecture must functionally integrate into living natural systems through a verified Two-Way Functional Exchange:**
1. **Nature → Building:** Explicitly state how the site's living natural systems actively run, cool, power, shelter, or support the building's performance (e.g., river-breeze evaporative cooling, wadi runoff harvesting, earth thermal mass, microclimate wind-catchers, agrivoltaic shade).
2. **Building → Nature:** Explicitly state how the building actively supports, protects, or regenerates the natural system (e.g., planted reed bio-filtration, native seed banking, migratory-bird habitat protection, zero-discharge recharge, urban heat-island cooling).

> **NEST Enforcement Rule:** Purely aesthetic or cosmetic "nature-inspired" concepts that lack verified two-way functional exchange are **rejected and reworked** during Stage 4 hypothesis testing. Every render brief must also visibly depict this physical integration.

## 2. Constraints

### Site, Environment & Physical Constraints
- **Site Selection:** Identify hidden sites across Egypt showing significant latent potential—verified directly through spatial data or predicted within a **2.5 km nearest-neighbor radius**.
- **Identifiable Boundaries:** Every site output must include a clearly defined site boundary.
- **Place of Construction:** Zone across land, **water**, or mountain terrain. Water zones are **scorable across all 3 engines** and must carry an explicit `water_zone_warning` advisory note explaining aquatic construction constraints and ecological precautions.
- **Ecological Integrity:** Protect natural systems and assess environmental impacts; flag any destructive intervention as **`Harmful to the Environment`**.
- **Emergency Reachability:** Pay particular attention to emergency access challenges (site accessibility).
- **Seasonal Changes:** Account for access conditions that vary throughout the year and seasonal resource availability (fluctuations in water or energy supply).
- **Long-Term Change & Future Resilience:** Evaluate multi-decade environmental shifts and long-term economic changes to ensure enduring resilience.

### Discovery & Design Principles
- **Look Beyond the Obvious:** Explore overlooked, unexplored, or underutilized landscapes and natural resources (including rare plants and biodiversity), recognizing hidden potential inside known places rather than relying only on famous spots or current recognition.
- **Search for Unusual Combinations:** Examine relationships and rare pairings across geology, climate, ecology, hydrology, and culture rather than isolated traits.
- **Identify Natural Processes:** Study how dynamic and seasonal forces (such as erosion, water/sediment flow, migration, and regeneration) operate so architecture can work with them.
- **Explore Environmental Contradictions:** Treat clashing conditions (heat vs. cooling, scarcity vs. seasonal water, fragility vs. activity) as sparks for innovation rather than automatic rejections.
- **Discover Ecological Relationships:** Map dependencies among species, habitats, water, soil, and climate, treating architecture as part of a larger ecosystem.
- **Recognize Site-Specific Conditions:** Let a location's distinct topography, orientation, microclimate, and context shape opportunities instead of forcing generic programs onto it.
- **Challenge Conventional Programs:** Move beyond familiar building types toward unconventional, distributed, adaptive, or evolving interventions shaped by the site.
- **Explore New Architecture–Nature Relationships:** Focus on interaction, coexistence, exchange, and adaptation over time rather than simply occupying land.
- **Pursue Innovation Through Investigation:** Test multiple hypotheses per site against precedents to find what is genuinely distinctive, meaningful, and environmentally responsible.
- **Think Beyond the Individual Site:** Examine how a site links with surrounding landscapes, ecological corridors, and wider networks.
- **Let Potential Emerge:** Stay open to new interpretations and functions instead of locking in a predetermined project before investigating the site.

### Data, Economic & Socio-Cultural Constraints
- **Missing Data & Evidence-Backed Estimation Policy:** Never make unvalidated guesses, and do not skip mandatory metrics (including financial projections such as Expected Revenue, IRR, ROI, and payback period). When direct site or market data is missing, actively search for valid proxy, regional, benchmark, or nearest-neighbor data (up to 2.5 km for spatial values) to **predict or estimate** the numbers, and **show all supporting evidence and calculations**. Every reported value must carry a mandatory **data provenance label** indicating how much was directly observed vs. predicted/estimated (e.g., `Observed [X% coverage]`, `Predicted [method & source]`, or `Estimated [benchmark, reasoning & calculation]`).
- **Nature-Linked Returns:** Prioritize opportunities where protecting ecological health supports long-term value.
- **Economic Value Study & Potential:** Evaluate investment feasibility, costs, revenue, and demand by investigating:
  - Potential users and visitors
  - Market demand
  - Competition and alternative destinations
  - Customer willingness to pay
  - Potential economic and funding partners
  - Market uncertainty
  - Mandatory financial projections: **Required Investment**, **Expected Revenue**, **Profitability**, **Payback Period**, **Internal Rate of Return (IRR)**, and **Return on Investment (ROI)**
- **Pilot-Scale Testing:** Consider opportunities that can be validated at pilot scale before major investment.
- **Flexible Future Uses:** Account for adaptability if market needs change.
- **Social, Human & Cultural Context:** Understand local communities, heritage, and stakeholders, connecting local knowledge, community livelihoods, cultural practices, and overlooked places across social, cultural, environmental, and economic value.

## 3. Context & Knowledge Domains

1. **Architecture Philosophy & Architectural Design Methods:** Case studies, theories, and architecture-based projects and concepts—including unconventional yet applicable ideas.
2. **Natural Systems & Scientific Phenomena:** Ecology, geology, hydrology, climatology, biology, physics, natural processes, environmental relationships, and unusual phenomena.
3. **Geographic & Site Intelligence:** GIS, satellite imagery, topography, land use, infrastructure, accessibility, land ownership, environmental mapping, and spatial relationships.
4. **Thinking & Mathematical Models:** Systems thinking, first-principles reasoning, abductive reasoning, Bayesian inference, causal analysis, anomaly detection, spatial statistics, network analysis, optimization, and scenario modeling.
5. **Economics & Business Models:** Development economics, financial feasibility, market demand, investment structures, revenue models, lifecycle costs, funding mechanisms, and conservation-linked economics.
6. **Research Methods & Evidence:** Literature review, scientific validation, source evaluation, uncertainty analysis, fieldwork, data acquisition, hypothesis testing, and reproducible documentation.
7. **Laws, Policies & Development Systems:** Egyptian planning regulations, environmental legislation, protected areas, ownership systems, institutional authorities, approval pathways, and development restrictions.
8. **People, Culture & Society:** Human behavior, social geography, local livelihoods, cultural heritage, stakeholder relationships, community needs, and participatory development.
9. **Smart Searching in Data:** Large architectural books are noisy if queried all at once. Tag and organize sources by target domain, and use external books as analytical tools and lenses—not as templates that force generic building types.

### Egypt-Specific & High-Significance Natural Assets
Iconic or uniquely Egyptian landscapes, endemic/migratory ecology, rare desert/arid/coastal/Nile/karst/mountain phenomena, high-conservation-value habitats, and sites of exceptional ecological, geological, or cultural-natural significance—evaluated for both **rarity/uniqueness** and **long-term stewardship value**:

1. **Uniquely / Mostly Egyptian Natural Phenomena & Settings:** Landscapes and processes strongly associated with or characteristic of Egypt (e.g., hyper-arid desert oases, wadi flash-flood/sediment dynamics, Red Sea/Mediterranean coastal & marine systems, Nile/Deltaic transitions, limestone/karst formations, and Sinai/Red Sea mountain microclimates).
2. **High Ecological & Scientific Significance:** Rare or fragile ecosystems, key biodiversity/migration corridors, unusual geological or hydrological features, or sites with high research, conservation, or ecological-restoration importance.
3. **High Cultural-Natural or Strategic Value:** Places where natural features carry strong regional identity, heritage-compatible meaning, or high long-term resilience and economic-conservation value—respecting NEST's nature-first mandate and flagging any destructive trade-off as **`Harmful to the Environment`**.

## 5. Scoring System of NEST

### Accessibility & Mobility
Each of the 5 indicators is equally weighted (`20%` each) and scored as: **High = `100`**, **Moderate = `50`**, **Low = `15`**.

| Indicator | High (`100`) | Moderate (`50`) | Low (`15`) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Distance to main road** | ≤ 2 km | 2–5 km | > 5 km |
| **Distance to settlement** | ≤ 5 km | 5–15 km | > 15 km |
| **Distance to public transport** | ≤ 1 km | 1–3 km | > 3 km |
| **Average access-route slope** | ≤ 5% | 5–10% | > 10% |
| **Emergency service travel** | ≤ 15 min | 15–30 min | > 30 min |

### Other Operational Categories *(TBD — Future Scoring Expansion)*
Quantitative scoring tables for **Land Plot**, **Utilities**, **Site Conditions**, **Policies**, and **Socio-Economic** are not yet defined. Until formal rubrics are added, agents must evaluate them using the structured tiers in Section 6 without inventing arbitrary numeric thresholds.

## 6. Operational Rules

### Tier 1: Hard Exclusions (Pass / Fail Gates)
Sites or proposals failing any gate below are excluded/rejected:
- **Financially Unfeasible Proposals:** Hard-rejected per Rule Precedence Priority 1.
- **Existing Dense Urban Fabric** *(Crystals & Cells Engines)*: Areas that do not offer the type of natural-site or natural-system adaptation opportunity being searched for.
- **Physically Inaccessible Land:** Excluded **only** when there is no feasible land, water, or air route to reach the site, or terrain conditions make construction/access physically impossible (e.g., sheer cliffs `> 45°` or unnavigable hazards). *(Remote sites with Low Accessibility = `15` are NOT auto-excluded if physically reachable.)*
- **Existing Major Infrastructure:** Areas occupied by immovable major infrastructure where intervention is not possible.

### Tier 2: Mandatory Advisory Flags (Retain & Surface Warning)
Sites or proposals triggering these conditions remain active but **must** carry an explicit flag in all outputs:
- **`Harmful to the Environment`:** Triggered whenever a financially feasible proposal degrades or destroys the natural asset; **must** be paired with a nature-safe feasible alternative if one exists.
- **`water_zone_warning`:** Triggered whenever a scorable cell/site intersects a water body across any of the 3 engines; must state aquatic construction constraints and ecological precautions.
- **`emergency_access_alert`:** Triggered when emergency service travel falls in the Low tier (`> 30 min`) or faces seasonal access disruption.
- **`policy_complexity`:** Triggered when locations require multi-authority coordination, heritage-preservation alignment, or complex/uncertain permitting.

### Tier 3: Configurable Parameters & Qualitative Evaluation Prompts

#### Land Plot
- **Suitable Plot Dimensions:** Free numeric input in **`m²` and Egyptian feddans** (`1 feddan = 4,200.83 m²`, no fixed min/max bounds) — either supplied by the user or inferred from the emergent program. All reported site areas must display both **`m²` and `feddans`**.
- **Footprint:** The site's capacity to host a **contiguous or distributed (multi-parcel / clustered)** footprint.
- **Land Transformation:** Consider how the site's usable area may change over time.
- **Future Expansion:** Consider whether the site can accommodate future growth.

#### Accessibility & Mobility
- **Hidden Connections:** Consider relationships between apparently unrelated places.
- **Regional Catalysts:** Pay attention to opportunities capable of benefiting surrounding areas.
- **Shared Mobility:** Consider transport connections shared with neighboring activities.

#### Utilities
- **Regional Gaps:** Consider locations missing a valuable activity or service.
- **Circular Resource Use:** Prioritize systems that reuse water, materials, or energy.
- **Low Utility Demand:** Give attention to projects with unusually low resource requirements.
- **Infrastructure Co-Benefits:** Consider systems that also provide value to local communities.

#### Site Conditions
- **Extreme Climate:** Pay attention to severe environmental conditions that could shape the opportunity.
- **Microclimate Differences:** Consider unusual local variations in temperature, wind, or humidity.
- **Seasonal Transformations:** Pay attention to changing physical and environmental conditions.
- **Natural Process Protection:** Prioritize avoiding disruption of important natural processes.
- **Environmental Recovery:** Consider opportunities that could support ecological restoration.

#### Policies
- **Temporary Permissions:** Pay attention to lawful temporary or reversible-use pathways.
- **Policy Transitions:** Consider documented changes in planning or land-use regulations.
- **Cross-Authority Coordination:** Pay attention to locations requiring multiple approvals.
- **Heritage-Compatible Uses:** Consider non-invasive activities compatible with heritage preservation and how to build better projects through collaboration.
- **Approval Complexity:** Give additional weight to the time, cost, and uncertainty of permits.

#### Socio-Economic
- **Phased Investment:** Consider projects that can begin small and expand over time.
- **Multiple Revenue Streams:** Consider sites capable of supporting complementary income sources.
- **Local Value Retention:** Consider how economic benefits can remain within nearby communities.
- **Economic Symbiosis:** Consider opportunities that complement existing industries and our nature-relied project.
- **Seasonal Livelihoods:** Pay attention to economic activities that vary throughout the year.
- **Unusual Revenue Models:** Consider alternatives to conventional property income.
