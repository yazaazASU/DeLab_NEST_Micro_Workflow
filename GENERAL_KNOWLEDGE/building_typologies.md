## 1. Residential
* **Efficiency Ratio (NSF/GSF):** 80%
* **Common Examples:** Single-family homes, apartment buildings, townhouses, dormitories, hotels, assisted living facilities.
* **Ideal Urban Context:** Quiet secondary streets, established urban neighborhoods, transit-adjacent residential districts, or suburban fringes buffered from heavy industry.
* **Access & Transit Needs:** Pedestrian-friendly street networks, local public transit access, low through-traffic, and safe walkability to daily amenities (parks, schools, groceries).
* **Site Resources ("What's in the Land"):** Sensory calm (low noise contours), natural views (waterfronts, greenery, horizons), favorable solar exposure and prevailing breezes, and an existing or emerging social fabric.
* **Why It Motivates the Building:** People choose and pay for quality of daily life. Sites that naturally offer privacy, daylight, acoustic quiet, and visual appeal directly drive livability, resident well-being, and long-term property value.

## 2. Commercial & Mercantile
* **Efficiency Ratio (NSF/GSF):** 78%
* **Common Examples:** Office towers, business parks, retail stores, shopping centers, restaurants, cafés, showrooms.
* **Ideal Urban Context:** Central Business Districts (CBDs), high-visibility arterial corridors, main streets, and major transit hubs.
* **Access & Transit Needs:** High-capacity public transit for offices; high foot-traffic exposure, street-level frontage, customer parking, and rear service/loading alleys for retail.
* **Site Resources ("What's in the Land"):** Human flow (foot traffic and commuter density), high street visibility, surrounding purchasing power, and proximity to an existing business or talent ecosystem.
* **Why It Motivates the Building:** Retail and commercial offices feed directly on human movement and economic density. A site that intercepts high daily traffic or clusters near existing commercial anchors generates revenue simply by being visible and accessible.

---

## 3. Institutional & Civic
* **Efficiency Ratio (NSF/GSF):** 70%
* **Common Examples:** Hospitals, clinics, schools, universities, research labs, libraries, museums, courthouses, municipal halls.
* **Ideal Urban Context:** Accessible urban nodes, dedicated campus districts, civic centers, or central neighborhood hubs.
* **Access & Transit Needs:** Multi-modal public accessibility; dedicated, unobstructed emergency-vehicle routes for hospitals; safe pedestrian catchment zones away from high-speed highways for schools.
* **Site Resources ("What's in the Land"):** Demographic service gaps (underserved local populations), cultural or historical prominence, visually symbolic urban axes, clean microclimates, and proximity to knowledge ecosystems (e.g., universities).
* **Why It Motivates the Building:** Civic and cultural venues thrive on sites that already carry symbolic, historical, or community gravity, while essential services like schools and clinics are directly motivated by gaps in local population coverage.

---

## 4. Industrial & Logistics
* **Efficiency Ratio (NSF/GSF):** 88%
* **Common Examples:** Factories, manufacturing plants, distribution warehouses, fulfillment hubs, cold storage, data centers.
* **Ideal Urban Context:** Urban periphery, highway interchanges, ports, rail corridors, or dedicated industrial parks buffered downwind from housing.
* **Access & Transit Needs:** Direct heavy-truck arterial and highway access without passing through local residential streets, rail/port links, and expansive turning aprons.
* **Site Resources ("What's in the Land"):** Flat, low-cost land, raw material outputs (agriculture, mining, timber), affordable workforce catchments, regional trade gateways, abundant process water, and heavy utility/energy corridors (or cool ambient climates for data centers).
* **Why It Motivates the Building:** Manufacturing and logistics are driven by supply-chain efficiency and operational cost. Locating near raw materials, labor pools, heavy utilities, and major transport arteries minimizes freight time and overhead.

---

## 5. Assembly & Recreational
* **Efficiency Ratio (NSF/GSF):** 72%
* **Common Examples:** Stadiums, arenas, theaters, convention centers, museums, gyms, resorts, parks, places of worship.
* **Ideal Urban Context:** Cultural districts, waterfronts, scenic natural landscapes, or dedicated entertainment zones with space for crowd dispersion.
* **Access & Transit Needs:** High-capacity mass transit stations paired with major roadway dispersion and large parking reservoirs to absorb sudden crowd arrival and exit surges.
* **Site Resources ("What's in the Land"):** Destination appeal, scenic or ecological landmarks (coastlines, mountains, parks), cultural heritage, natural amphitheater topography, and vibrant nightlife or tourism energy.
* **Why It Motivates the Building:** Recreational and assembly venues capitalize on sites that already hold emotional, scenic, or cultural gravity—turning an inherently memorable place into a magnet for large gatherings and destination visits.

---

## 6. Transportation & Infrastructure
* **Efficiency Ratio (NSF/GSF):** 70%
* **Common Examples:** Airports, train stations, bus terminals, seaports, parking hubs, solar/wind farms, water treatment facilities, utility substations.
* **Ideal Urban Context:** Intermodal junctions, city edges (airports), waterfronts (ports), or strategic utility corridors separated from incompatible uses.
* **Access & Transit Needs:** Direct anchoring to rail lines, expressways, flight paths, shipping lanes, or high-voltage transmission and pipeline grids.
* **Site Resources ("What's in the Land"):** Geographic convergence points and network pinch points (where movement naturally bottlenecks), plus harvestable physical flows such as high solar irradiance, prevailing wind corridors, deep natural harbors, or elevation/watershed gradients.
* **Why It Motivates the Building:** Infrastructure is dictated by network efficiency and resource harvesting. Transit hubs resolve natural movement bottlenecks, while energy and water facilities are sited wherever natural flows can be captured and distributed with the least loss.

## 7. Mixed-Use (Composite of 2+ Compatible Typologies)
* **Efficiency Ratio (NSF/GSF):** 72%
* **Definition:** Any development that combines **two or more** of the six primary typologies above on the same site, provided every pair of combined uses is mutually compatible (e.g., Residential + Commercial + Institutional, or Industrial + Transportation).
* **Common Examples:** Retail podiums with residential or office towers above, transit-oriented developments (TODs), civic-commercial hubs, cultural-hospitality waterfronts, and intermodal logistics-transit complexes.
* **Ideal Urban Context:** High-value urban infill sites, transit nodes, main-street corridors, and multi-function districts where two or more compatible urban energies overlap strongly.
* **Why It Motivates the Building:** Combining compatible uses maximizes land efficiency and creates self-sustaining urban activity—pairing complementary daytime/nighttime or functional flows without introducing land-use conflicts (such as heavy industry next to housing).

## 8. Hydrology & Waterfront
* **Efficiency Ratio (NSF/GSF):** 76%
* **Nature-First Spatial Force Definition:** Evaluates waterfronts, rivers, lakes, wadis, canals, drainage corridors, and water-efficiency interfaces where architecture can harvest, filter, cool with, or restore aquatic and hydrological systems.

## 9. Solar & Climate Intensity
* **Efficiency Ratio (NSF/GSF):** 78%
* **Nature-First Spatial Force Definition:** Evaluates high solar irradiance (`GHI`), extreme cooling degree hours (`CDH`), evaporative demand (`ETo`), and thermal contrast zones where climatic mega-canopies, earth-sheltered sanctuaries, floating/agrivoltaic arrays, and passive cooling engines turn harsh climate into a primary resource.

## 10. Biodiversity & Ecology
* **Efficiency Ratio (NSF/GSF):** 75%
* **Nature-First Spatial Force Definition:** Evaluates observed GBIF flora, avian, mammal, and species hotspots, nature reserves, wetlands, and acoustic quiet zones where architecture acts as an active habitat-restoration nursery, seed bank, or low-impact eco-scientific research anchor.

## 11. Heritage & Culture Gravity
* **Efficiency Ratio (NSF/GSF):** 74%
* **Nature-First Spatial Force Definition:** Evaluates UNESCO World Heritage sites, archaeological monuments, cultural places, and scenic viewsheds where low-profile, earth-sheltered, or sensitively aligned civic and eco-cultural landmarks enter into dialogue with Egypt's heritage.

## 12. Civic & Service Deficit
* **Efficiency Ratio (NSF/GSF):** 73%
* **Nature-First Spatial Force Definition:** Evaluates populated settlements and rural/regional communities underserved by healthcare (`health_facilities` gap) and education/youth infrastructure (`education_facilities` gap) where climate-resilient clinics, STEM academies, and shaded civic commons bridge regional deficits.

## 13. Corridor & Network Connectivity
* **Efficiency Ratio (NSF/GSF):** 75%
* **Nature-First Spatial Force Definition:** Evaluates primary highways, rail spines, river/lake/sea ports, and settlement convergence nodes where multi-modal eco-ports, zero-carbon solar cold-chain logistics, and regional visitor gateways stitch fragmented territories together.
